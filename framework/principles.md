# Engineering Principles

## 1. Think before coding

Implementation starts only after the problem, constraints, expected outcome,
and affected systems are understood.

## 2. Preserve before replacing

Existing behavior, contracts, integrations, and validated architecture should
be preserved unless a change is justified by measurable benefit.

## 3. Separate knowledge from execution

Persistent facts belong in memory. Reusable expertise belongs in skills.
Execution behavior belongs in agents and workflows.

## 4. Prefer explicit contracts

Agents, memories, workflows, and tools must declare what they consume, what
they produce, and what they are not allowed to do.

## 5. Optimize for maintainability

Clarity, bounded responsibilities, observability, tests, and documentation
take priority over clever implementations.

## 6. Treat quality as a system

Correctness alone is insufficient. Production quality also includes security,
accessibility, performance, resilience, maintainability, and user experience.

## 7. Make decisions traceable

Important architecture decisions must be documented through ADRs or equivalent
records.

## 8. Reuse before creating

Before creating new logic, agents must inspect existing components, skills,
patterns, utilities, and integrations.

## 9. Prefer incremental delivery

Large changes should be decomposed into reversible, testable stages.

## 10. Document as part of delivery

A change is incomplete when its behavior, contracts, operational impact, or
architecture implications are undocumented.
